# What is best for this child

**Twenty approaches. Twelve pass the viability gate. Four are aimed at the right variable.
Those four split on a mechanistic question nobody has measured — and the experiment that
separates them costs less than any of them.**

*Producer: `scripts/mva/viability_gate.py`. Trial records queried directly from
ClinicalTrials.gov, 21 Oct 2026, not taken from reviews.*

## The four that survive both gates

All obtainable now, all aimed at BubR1 abundance:

| approach | targets | evidence in *BUB1B* | route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NAD⁺ precursor (NMN/NR)** | **degradation** | **yes** — NMN raised BubR1 abundance in vivo; SIRT2 extended lifespan in the hypomorphic mouse | available; n=1 precedent at NIH in Li-Fraumeni |
| **Arimoclomol** | **folding** | none | **open expanded-access programme** (NCT04316637, AVAILABLE, 13 US sites); Phase 3 in ALS |
| **4-phenylbutyrate** | **folding** | none | approved in children for urea cycle disorders; trialled for a destabilising missense in MCAD deficiency (NCT06069375) |
| Combination (corrector + degradation modulator) | both | none | CFTR precedent: single agents failed, the combination is what was approved |

## Why the decision is not a drug

The four do not differ mainly in strength. They differ in **which defect they assume**:

- If p.Asn1002Lys is a **folding** defect, a chaperone helps and a degradation modulator is
  aimed past the problem.
- If it is a **degradation** defect, the reverse.

**We have never measured which it is.** Everything in this project has assumed folding,
on the strength of a buried charge at 13.9% RSA and a lost hydrogen bond to Trp978 — a
prediction from an axis validated in three other proteins and never in this one.

So the answer to "what is best for this child" is:

> **The N1002R lane.** One construct, one extra lane on a western blot already designed.
> Arginine matches lysine's charge and exceeds its bulk but cannot accept ubiquitin. If K
> is unstable and R is stable, the defect is degradation and the NAD⁺ arm is right. If both
> are unstable, it is folding and the chaperones are right.
>
> That single experiment selects among four obtainable drugs. Nothing else we could do
> this year has that leverage.

## If forced to name one today

**Folding defect → arimoclomol.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **There is an open expanded-access programme.** For a child with an ultra-rare disease
   and no trial to enrol in, a live early-access route is not a detail — it is the
   difference between a plan and a wish.
2. **Phase 3 human safety exists**, across ALS, inclusion body myositis and Niemann-Pick C.
3. **It does not touch the degradation clock.** This is the mechanistic point, and it is
   the one that changed our ranking.

**Degradation defect → NAD⁺ precursor.** It remains the only candidate with evidence in
this gene, which is not a small thing.

## The shift this forced, stated plainly

We previously recommended the NAD⁺ route as the single viable option. That was right given
the fifteen approaches then on the table and is **no longer the whole answer**.

The waveform analysis established that BubR1 must be *destroyed* at anaphase for the cell
to divide, and that Choi 2009 showed an undegradable BubR1 markedly delays segregation.
**The NAD⁺ route works by slowing that same destruction.** It is the only one of the four
whose mechanism of action and principal risk are the same event.

A folding chaperone has no such conflict: it helps the protein fold, so less is degraded in
the first place, without interfering with the destruction that has to happen on schedule.

**On that reasoning arimoclomol is the mechanistically safer of the two**, and NAD⁺ keeps
its place only because it is the one with gene-specific evidence. Honest summary: *they are
co-leads, distinguished by a hypothesis one experiment settles.*

## What we are not saying

We are not proposing that anyone give this child anything. No dose, no schedule, no route.
4-PBA in particular carries a confound we will not paper over — it is a weak HDAC inhibitor,
and we argued HDAC inhibition is unpredictable here because K250 acetylation stabilises
BubR1 while K668 acetylation destabilises it. A drug that does both at once is not a clean
test of the folding hypothesis.

And the proposal remains the order of operations the Li-Fraumeni n=1 already validated:
**measure the deficit in the patient's own cells, test whether the agent rescues it in
those same cells, and only then consider a single-patient study with a mechanistic
readout.** The external check is not our opinion — it is whether the agent rescues the
deficit in that child's fibroblasts.

## What does not depend on any of this

**280 of 2,536 alleles lose the transcript.** Of twenty approaches, the eight that could
reach them are all blocked by the viability gate. That boundary has now survived five
changes of technology, a viability gate that overturned our own top pick, and an expansion
from fifteen candidates to twenty.

The eligibility map is the only thing here that is both useful and available today. It is
what we are submitting; the drug is a consequence of it, and a conditional one.
